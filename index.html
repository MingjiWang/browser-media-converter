Browser Media Converter (FFmpeg.wasm)

Convert audio/video locally in your browser (no backend). If something fails, the Worker logs will show why.

How to use
  1. Click Load Engine (first load may take time).
  2. Drop a file (audio or video) or click the drop area.
  3. Choose output format + preset (or advanced settings).
  4. Click Convert and download the result.

Some formats/codecs are experimental in FFmpeg.wasm builds. If one fails, try MP4/WebM for video, WAV/MP3/AAC/FLAC for audio.

            Advanced settings (what each does)

            

              These map to FFmpeg flags. You can copy the “Exec:” line from the Log and run it on desktop FFmpeg too.
              
    
                
  • Audio bitrate (-b:a): for lossy audio. Higher → larger file.
    • 
                
  • Sample rate (-ar): resample audio. Common: 44100, 48000.
    • 
                
  • Channels (-ac): force mono/stereo. Mono reduces size.
    • 
                
  • FPS (-r): force frame rate. Higher fps increases work/size.
    • 
                
  • Scale (-vf scale=W:H): resize video/GIF. Use 1280:-1 to keep aspect ratio.
    • 
                
  • Codec (-c:v): encoder choice. If output fails, try mpeg4.
    • 
                
  • CRF (-crf): quality knob (lower=better) for codecs that support it (esp. H.264/VP9).
    • 
              

              Quick recipes:

              • Smaller MP4: scale=1280:-1, crf=26, fps=30

              • Good audio: mp3 320k or aac 256k, 48kHz

              • If encode fails: switch video codec to mpeg4 or output to WebM.
            


            
Audio

            

              

                
Bitrate (kbps)

                
                

                  Sets audio bitrate (-b:a). Only matters for lossy formats (MP3/AAC/OGG/Opus).

                  Examples: 128 (small), 192 (balanced), 320 (high).
                

              

              

                
Sample rate (Hz)

                
                

                  Resamples audio (-ar). Common: 44100 for music, 48000 for video.
                

              

              

                
Channels

                
                

                  Forces channel count (-ac). Mono is good for speech/podcasts.
                

              

            


            
Video

            

              

                
FPS

                
                

                  Forces frame rate (-r). Common: 24, 30, 60.
                

              

              

                
Scale (w:-1)

                
                

                  Resize video (-vf scale=...). Use width:-1 to preserve aspect ratio.
                

              

              

                
Codec

                
                

                  Selects the video encoder (-c:v). If H.264 fails in this build, try mpeg4 or WebM.
                

              

              

                
CRF

                
                

                  Constant Rate Factor (-crf) controls quality for supported codecs (H.264/VP9).

                  Lower = better quality + larger file. Typical H.264: 18–28.
                

              

            


            
GIF

            

              

                
GIF FPS

                
                

                  GIF frame rate (-r). Lower fps makes much smaller GIFs.
                

              

              

                
GIF scale (w:-1)

                
                

                  Resize GIF (-vf scale=...). Try 480:-1 or 640:-1 to reduce size.
                

              

            


            

              Leave advanced fields blank to keep defaults. If you switch preset back to Standard/High, advanced values are ignored.
